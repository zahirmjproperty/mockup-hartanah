5 · Why the earlier versions failed

The original brief stored the gradient in a custom property:

--sweep: conic-gradient(from var(--za) …);
background: var(--sweep);          /* ← var() inside a custom property is
                                      resolved at DECLARATION time */

By the time the pseudo-element inherited --sweep, the inner var(--za) had already been fixed to its initial value. Animating --za afterwards changed nothing, so all that was left was a uniformly glowing ring — a pulse, never a travelling light. Writing the gradient inside the pseudo-element rule keeps var(--za) live, which is what makes the light move.

The other half is that the ring must not be a rotating element: a rotating square cannot follow a rounded border, so the glow always breaks at the corners. Animating the gradient angle instead leaves the ring perfectly aligned at every frame.

6 · Accessible by default

Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the animation stops and the gradient is pinned to a static angle — the button still reads as gold, with no motion. Motion is never the only thing carrying meaning.
